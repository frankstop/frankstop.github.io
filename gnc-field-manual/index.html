CH 00

The whole system in one picture

Estimation turns imperfect evidence into a belief about the world. Tracking manages those beliefs over time. Fusion combines information. GNC uses the resulting state to decide and act.

Sensors
Measurements
State estimates
Guidance plan
Control action
Navigation

Where am I, how am I moving, and how uncertain is that answer?

Navigation estimates the vehicle state from inertial sensors, GPS/GNSS, cameras, star trackers, radar, altimeters, or other measurements.

Guidance

Where should I go next?

Guidance converts mission objectives and constraints into a reference trajectory, waypoint, attitude, velocity, or intercept command.

Control

What actuator command makes reality follow the reference?

Control closes the loop using thrust, steering, torque, control surfaces, reaction wheels, brakes, or motors.

The expert separation

A tracker estimating another object is not automatically “navigation.” Navigation estimates the host vehicle’s state. Both use similar estimation machinery, but their frames, dynamics, sensors, safety cases, and failure consequences differ.

CH 01

A state is a compact prediction machine

The state contains the minimum variables needed to predict future behavior under your model. It is not every fact you know about an object.

Example: 1D motion

Position + velocity

x = [ position, velocity ]ᵀ
xₖ₊₁ = Fxₖ + Buₖ + wₖ
zₖ = Hxₖ + vₖ

F propagates dynamics. B maps known inputs. H maps state into sensor space. w and v represent process and measurement uncertainty.

Units matter

Covariance is geometry, not a confidence vibe

The covariance matrix P contains variances on the diagonal and cross-covariances off diagonal. Its ellipsoid shows directions of high and low uncertainty.

P = E[(x − x̂)(x − x̂)ᵀ]
P₁₁ → m²   P₂₂ → (m/s)²
TermEngineering meaningTypical mistake
State xThe latent variables the model propagatesAdding unobservable states because they sound useful
Measurement zWhat the sensor actually reportsTreating a derived detection as raw truth
Process noise QUnmodeled dynamics accumulated per predictionCalling it “sensor noise” or tuning it without units
Measurement noise RUncertainty of observations in measurement spaceUsing a constant value across range and geometry
FrameCoordinates and reference in which values are expressedFusing ENU, NED, body, sensor, or map states as if identical
Observability questionIf two different internal states can produce exactly the same measurement history, the sensor/model combination cannot distinguish them. No filter tuning fixes an unobservable system.
CH 02

Filtering is recursive Bayesian inference

Start with a belief, predict it through dynamics, compare the predicted observation with the real observation, then update the belief.

Predict

Prior: what the model expected

p(xₖ | z₁:ₖ₋₁) = ∫ p(xₖ | xₖ₋₁)
· p(xₖ₋₁ | z₁:ₖ₋₁) dxₖ₋₁

Prediction moves both the mean and uncertainty forward. Uncertainty generally grows because the future contains disturbances you did not model exactly.

Correct

Posterior: what you believe after seeing data

p(xₖ | z₁:ₖ) ∝ p(zₖ | xₖ)
· p(xₖ | z₁:ₖ₋₁)

The likelihood scores which states could have produced the observation. The posterior becomes the prior for the next cycle.

Three estimates experts keep distinct

Filtering

Estimate the current state using data through now: xₖ | z₁:ₖ. Real-time systems live here.

Prediction

Estimate a future state: xₖ₊ₕ | z₁:ₖ. Guidance and collision checking consume this.

Smoothing

Estimate a past state using later data: xₖ | z₁:T. Better accuracy, but requires latency or offline processing.

Gaussian does not mean “the world is Gaussian.”

It means the algorithm represents belief using a mean and covariance and often assumes Gaussian disturbances. Multimodal ambiguity, hard constraints, clutter, missed detections, and nonlinear geometry can violate that representation.

CH 03

The Kalman filter, without ritual

For linear dynamics and observations with Gaussian, white, correctly modeled noise, the Kalman filter is the minimum mean-square-error estimator. The equations are a cycle of propagation, comparison, weighting, and correction.

Time update

Move the estimate and covariance through the model

x̂ₖ⁻ = Fₖx̂ₖ₋₁⁺ + Bₖuₖ
Pₖ⁻ = FₖPₖ₋₁⁺Fₖᵀ + Qₖ

The superscript minus means “before this measurement.” Q deliberately enlarges uncertainty for dynamics the model cannot predict.

Residual in sensor space

Ask how surprising the measurement is

yₖ = zₖ − Hₖx̂ₖ⁻
Sₖ = HₖPₖ⁻Hₖᵀ + Rₖ

The innovation y is not meaningful without S. A 5-meter residual can be ordinary or catastrophic depending on predicted measurement uncertainty.

Optimal linear weighting

Convert innovation into a state correction

Kₖ = Pₖ⁻HₖᵀSₖ⁻¹

Large R usually lowers measurement influence. Large predicted uncertainty usually raises it. Avoid explicit matrix inversion in production; solve a linear system using a numerically stable factorization.

Measurement update

Correct the mean and reduce uncertainty

x̂ₖ⁺ = x̂ₖ⁻ + Kₖyₖ
Pₖ⁺ = (I − KₖHₖ)Pₖ⁻(I − KₖHₖ)ᵀ + KₖRₖKₖᵀ

The second line is the Joseph form. It better preserves symmetry and positive semidefiniteness under finite-precision arithmetic.

Choose the filter to match the representation

MethodUse whenMain limit
Linear KFF and H are linear; uncertainty is adequately GaussianCannot represent nonlinear mappings directly
EKFModels are mildly nonlinear and differentiableLinearization can bias or diverge; Jacobians must be right
UKF / sigma-pointNonlinearity is stronger; derivatives are awkwardMore evaluations and tuning; still unimodal
Particle filterBelief is nonlinear, non-Gaussian, or multimodalComputational cost and sample degeneracy
Information filterSparse systems or distributed fusion favor information formRecovering covariance can be costly
IMMA target switches among motion modelsModel-set design and transition probabilities matter

NIS: measurement consistency

NIS = yᵀS⁻¹y

Over many samples, compare NIS with chi-square bounds for the measurement dimension. Persistently high: uncertainty may be too small, noise non-Gaussian, data misassociated, or the model wrong. Persistently low: uncertainty may be inflated.

NEES: state consistency

NEES = (x − x̂)ᵀP⁻¹(x − x̂)

NEES requires ground truth, so it is usually a simulation or instrumented-test metric. A low RMSE filter can still be dangerously inconsistent if P claims unjustified certainty.

CH 04

A tracker is more than a filter

A filter updates one hypothesis. A multi-object tracker must decide which observations belong to which objects, when a track becomes credible, and when it should die.

Transformtime + frame

Associateassign evidence

Gating

Use Mahalanobis distance in innovation space:

d² = yᵀS⁻¹y

A chi-square threshold sets a statistically interpretable gate. Rectangular distance thresholds ignore covariance geometry.

Association

NN: closest valid hit.
GNN: global one-to-one assignment.
JPDA: weighted association probabilities.
MHT: delays commitment by maintaining hypotheses.

Track management

Tentative tracks require confirmation logic. Confirmed tracks coast through limited misses. Deletion balances false persistence against premature loss. Scores should use detection probability and clutter density where possible.

CH 05

Fusion: combine information without counting it twice

Fusion is easy only when errors are independent, timestamps align, frames match, biases are handled, and every covariance means what it claims. Real systems violate all five.

Centralized

Detection-level fusion

Send measurements to one tracker. It retains the most information and can model each sensor directly, but demands bandwidth, timing discipline, and central computation.

Decentralized

Track-to-track fusion

Local trackers send states and covariances. This reduces bandwidth and isolates subsystems, but cross-correlation can make naive fusion overconfident.

Hybrid

Selected raw + local tracks

Useful when some sensors are tightly coupled and others arrive as vendor tracks. Interface contracts become part of the estimator design.

Independent two-estimate fusion

Pᶠ = (P₁⁻¹ + P₂⁻¹)⁻¹
x̂ᶠ = Pᶠ(P₁⁻¹x̂₁ + P₂⁻¹x̂₂)

The independence trap

If two local tracks share process noise, prior information, map data, or earlier fused outputs, their errors are correlated. Applying the independent formula reuses common information and produces covariance that is too small. Use known cross-covariance, a decorrelation architecture, information bookkeeping, or conservative methods such as covariance intersection when correlation is unknown.

CH 06

Close the loop: Guidance, Navigation & Control

GNC is an information-and-action loop. The estimator’s latency, bias, uncertainty, and frame conventions directly shape control stability and mission performance.

Guidance

Reference generation

Path planning, trajectory optimization, intercept laws, waypoints, constraint handling. Output: a desired state or trajectory as a function of time.

Navigation

State determination

Sensor calibration, propagation, measurement update, fault detection, integrity. Output: state, covariance, time, frame, and health.

Control

Reference tracking

Feedback and feedforward turn state error into actuator commands. Output: force, torque, steering, throttle, or motor commands.

Control vocabulary you need in a design review

ConceptMeaningWhy the estimator cares
StabilityWhether disturbances decay or growDelay, bias, and noise can erode margins or excite the loop
ControllabilityWhether inputs can move the system through required statesNo estimator can overcome missing actuator authority
ObservabilityWhether measurements reveal the internal stateUnobservable modes drift regardless of controller quality
BandwidthHow quickly a loop can respondEstimation rate and latency must support it
SaturationPhysical actuator command limitCauses nonlinear behavior and possible integrator windup
RobustnessTolerance to uncertainty and model errorCovariance alone does not cover unknown bias or bad models

PID

u = Kₚe + Kᵢ∫e dt + Kᴅ de/dt

Practical and interpretable. Derivative action amplifies measurement noise, so state estimates or filtered derivatives matter. Anti-windup matters under saturation.

LQR / state feedback

u = −Kx̂
J = ∫(xᵀQcx + uᵀRcu)dt

Balances state error against control effort. With a Kalman estimator and linear assumptions, LQR + KF forms LQG, but robustness still needs analysis.

Latency is part of the plantA beautifully tuned estimator that is 150 ms late supplies a precise estimate of the wrong time. Propagate estimates to the control epoch, timestamp at acquisition, and measure end-to-end delay distributions.
CH 07

What experts test before they trust

A convincing demo is not validation. Build evidence across truth data, Monte Carlo trials, stress cases, numerical checks, interfaces, timing, and closed-loop behavior.

Estimator health checks

✓P remains symmetric and positive semidefinite
✓Innovations are approximately zero mean and temporally white
✓NIS/NEES distributions match statistical bounds over ensembles
✓Bias, frame, units, time offset, and sign errors are injected deliberately
✓Dropouts, outliers, saturation, clutter, and asynchronous updates are covered

System-level checks

✓Requirements define accuracy by axis, regime, percentile, and horizon
✓Latency is measured acquisition-to-actuation, not just algorithm runtime
✓Monte Carlo seeds and configurations are reproducible
✓Hardware-in-the-loop covers quantization, scheduling, and interfaces
✓Fault detection has measured false-alarm and missed-detection rates

A disciplined tuning order

StepActionEvidence
1Verify equations, units, frames, Jacobians, discretization, and timestampsUnit tests and hand-checkable cases
2Calibrate sensor R from stationary and regime-specific dataResidual distributions and Allan/deviation analysis where relevant
3Derive Q from disturbance models or acceleration spectral densityPrediction error across horizons
4Check innovation mean, whiteness, NIS, and NEESEnsemble statistics, not a single run
5Tune gates and track logic against clutter and missed detectionsFalse tracks, continuity, ID switches, OSPA/GOSPA
6Validate closed-loop margins and mission outcomesSIL, HIL, field tests, fault campaigns

Debug in this order

Time → frame → units → sign → model → noise. Teams often tune Q and R around a timestamp or coordinate transform bug. That can make one dataset look better while making the estimator fundamentally wrong.

CHECKPOINT

Can you reason like the reviewer?

Choose the strongest answer. Explanations appear immediately.

1. Position RMSE is low, but NEES is consistently above the upper chi-square bound. What is the best conclusion?

NEES normalizes actual error by reported covariance. High NEES means the estimator claims more certainty than its errors justify.

2. Two radar tracks use the same motion model and previously received the same fused track. Can you fuse them as independent?

Shared priors and earlier fused outputs create correlation. Ignoring it double counts information and shrinks covariance unjustifiably.

3. A constant-velocity filter lags every hard turn. What is the first model-level hypothesis?

Maneuvers violate the model. More process uncertainty, an acceleration state, or an IMM may be appropriate after time/frame bugs are excluded.

4. Which quantity is correct for statistical gating of a measurement?

The innovation and its covariance define distance in measurement space, accounting for direction-dependent uncertainty.

5. The estimator is accurate but 200 ms late. The controller oscillates. What matters?

Delay changes closed-loop dynamics. A precise stale estimate can destabilize or degrade the controller.

6. Innovations have a strong repeating pattern around zero. What does that suggest?

Good innovations should be not only near zero mean but also approximately white. Autocorrelation signals model, timing, or bias structure the filter missed.
